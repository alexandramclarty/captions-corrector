Settings

Short additions and removals are applied automatically. A longer run of unmatched words usually means the two files drifted out of step, so those are left alone and flagged for you.

Your captions have a house line width. Re-wrapping corrected cues any narrower would split them into two lines while every untouched cue stayed on one, making the corrected ones stand out on screen.

Timings stay fixed, so adding words speeds up the reading rate. Only cues your edits pushed over the line are flagged — a cue that was already dense and got no denser is left alone.

Word turns your quotes and apostrophes curly as you type. Curly characters are the ones that garble when a player misreads a file's encoding, and leaving them in would also mark every contraction in the file as a change. This converts them back on the way out: ' and " for curly quotes, a spaced - for an em dash, a bare - for an en dash, ... for an ellipsis. Your Word document is untouched.

Turn this on if your transcript has lines like Maria Chen: that shouldn't go into the captions.

Changed cues

Everything runs in your browser. Neither file is uploaded anywhere.

No language model is involved. The comparison uses Myers diff, the same algorithm behind git diff. Every word written to the output comes from one of the two files you provided, and that is checked before the download is offered.
